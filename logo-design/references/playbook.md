# Icon and logo playbook

Distilled from the 2026-10-03 research run: masters, YouTube transcripts, courses and Apple HIG. The raw quotes are in `principles-notes.md`.

## Principles

1. **A logo identifies. It does not explain.** "A logo derives its meaning from the quality of the thing it symbolizes" (Paul Rand). "The period at the end of a sentence, not the sentence itself" (Sagi Haviv).
2. **A new mark is an empty vessel.** Make the vessel the right shape for the meaning it will hold (Michael Bierut).
3. **Appropriate, distinctive, simple** (Haviv). Appropriate means the right personality. The less a mark says, the better.
4. **One feature** (David Airey). Can you name it in five words?
5. **Shape first, black and white first.** Colour is secondary (Airey).
6. **Push abstraction until just before it stops reading** (Saul Bass).
7. **Simple, but not boring.** An idea that makes you think again (Bass).
8. **Quiet stands out.** "In a world where everybody screams, silence is noticeable" (Massimo Vignelli).
9. **An icon is a road sign, not an illustration.** "A salient detail or two, but don't lard it" (Susan Kare).
10. **For things that cannot be drawn, borrow from an existing sign system** (Kare's "undo"). Use symbol dictionaries, hobo signs, kanji, engineering symbols and music notation.
11. **Never draw a specific piece of technology.** It dates the mark (Kare).
12. **Look outside tech** for references: folk art, signage, tools (Kare).
13. **Use the subject's own material.** Bierut's MAD Museum logo came from the building's geometry. Raycast chose a keycap, the thing every user touches.
14. **App icons are not logos.** The five qualities are scalability, recognizability, consistency with the app, uniqueness, and no words (Michael Flarup).
15. **Remove details until the concept starts to break, then stop** (Flarup).
16. **Apple HIG (June 2026):**
    - One essential concept, made from a minimal number of shapes.
    - No thin lines and no sharp corners.
    - Let the system add the glass, shadows and highlights.
    - The icon must stay recognizable in the default, dark, tinted and clear appearances.
17. **Make rules, then work inside them.** Teenage Engineering: "black, white, one colour, it always works". The accent colour has one meaning.
18. **Design, not styling.** Every choice needs a reason (Jesper Kouthoofd).
19. **Absence can be the mark.** Nothing's first logo idea was a hole. To be distinct, absence needs a second layer, such as a decision or a sense of permanence.
20. **It is never love at first sight** (Haviv, Geismar). Meaning builds through use. Do not poll people on whether they like it.
21. **Hidden negative-space tricks are not noticed without being told.** FedEx study, PMC10317936. The bare shape must work alone.

## Concept process

1. **Soak.** Read everything about the product and write down the founder's offhand phrases.
2. **Write four lists:** belief, truths, visual language, anti-brief.
3. **Make word lists in columns:** what it does, what it believes, the name, and its abstract qualities. Ask how each word looks.
4. **Search sign systems** for a glyph that already means one of those words.
5. **Combine across columns.** Look for one double reading, not a pile of puns.
6. **Make a large number of quick, ugly ideas.** At least 100 thumbnails in 30–60 second bursts, black only.
7. **Glance at the whole grid and cull to 6–10.**
8. **Block out the survivors** in grayscale at 16px and 1024px.
9. **Add the accent last,** with one job.
10. **Pin them up overnight,** critique them, then present 3–4 in context.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| One idea | Named in five words |
| Simple | 3 shapes or fewer; reads at 16px and at 1 inch on paper |
| Silhouette | Squint or blur test |
| Memorable | Show it for 5 seconds; the next day the person can redraw it |
| Distinctive | Put it in a grid of 20 competitor icons; run a reverse image search |
| Mono-first | Works with the accent removed and in Apple's tinted and clear modes |
| Platform | Light and dark Dock; light and dark Chrome toolbar; no thin strokes |
| Appropriate | Three strangers each give one adjective; check it is the one you want |
| Durable | No gradient, glow or glass baked in; would it look fine in 2036? |
| Not cliché | Passes the anti-brief; not on page one of "<concept> symbol" image results |

## Clichés seen in 2026

- **Focus and attention:** eye, brain, target, ripples, magnifier, hourglass or clock, crescent moon.
- **Wellbeing:** lotus, leaf or tree, gem, sun, zen stones.
- **Control:** padlock, shield, key, chain, power button, phone with a slash, "no" sign.
- **Absence, already taken in the focus and blocker category:** a dash (Blank Spaces), a ring with a gap (one sec), empty brackets (Brick).
- **AI-era:** sparkle, gradient ring with a hole, dark tile with a purple glow, glassy 3D blobs.

## Case studies of abstract marks

- **CND / peace symbol:** semaphore letters combined with a human gesture.
- **Chase octagon:** meaning taught through repetition.
- **Tate:** attention shown as a behaviour.
- **Are.na:** two asterisks; restraint is the message.
- **MUJI:** emptiness is the brand.
- **Signal:** a dashed boundary instead of a lock.
- **Proton:** a cliché cropped until it becomes abstract.
- **Conservation International:** a circle and a line.
- **Deutsche Bank:** growth inside a safe frame.
- **SBB clock:** a built-in pause before a decisive move.

## Where the first run landed (a focus app, 2026-10-03)

The strongest directions at 16px were fermata (hold), electrical ground, diode (one-way) and frame struck once.

What failed:
- Ratchet read as a saw blade.
- Stet read as a loading indicator.
- Plumb bob read as a map pin.
- Signal-from-noise was too busy.
- Valve read as a bowtie.
- Level read as a toggle switch.
